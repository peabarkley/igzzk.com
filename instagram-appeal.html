Instagram appeal: how to appeal a disabled or restricted account

7 min readUpdated October 2026

Your account was disabled or restricted. Instagram didn't tell you why — just a notice that you violated its rules. You want to appeal, but you're not sure where to start or what to submit.

Here's the official appeal path, explained in plain English. What you submit depends on whether your account was disabled (you can't log in) or restricted (you can log in, but actions are blocked).

The short answer

If your account was disabled, open the Instagram app and try to log in. You'll see a notice with an "Appeal" or "Disagree with Decision" button. Tap it and follow the on-screen steps. View source ↗

If you can still log in but specific actions are blocked, go to Settings → Account Status to see what's restricted and whether you can request a review. View source ↗

First, find your situation

The appeal path differs depending on what happened. Find the row that matches your situation:

Your situation The appeal route
You see "Your account has been disabled" when you try to log in In-app appeal (below)
You can log in, but you can't follow, comment, or like Account Status review
Your post was removed or you got a violation warning Violation warning appeal
Your account was restricted for "data scraping" Account restriction page
Someone else has access to your account Account recovery

Route 1: In-app appeal (disabled account)

This is the preferred path. Instagram's Help Center says if you believe your account was disabled by mistake, you can request a review by logging in and following the on-screen instructions. View source ↗

  • Open the Instagram app and try to log in normally.
  • When you see the notice that your account has been disabled, tap "Appeal" or "Disagree with Decision" (the exact wording depends on your app version).
  • Enter your login credentials if prompted.
  • Follow the on-screen instructions to confirm your identity. This often involves:
    • Entering the email address on your account
    • Submitting a video selfie (good lighting, face clearly visible, follow the head-turn prompts exactly)
    • Confirming a code sent to your email
  • Submit the appeal. Take a screenshot of the confirmation for your records.

What Instagram says about video selfies

Instagram uses selfie videos because photos and ID cards can be digitally altered. During review, you won't be able to access your account. The video is never shown on Instagram and is deleted within 30 days. View source ↗

If Instagram can't verify you from the video, you can submit a new one and they'll review it again.

How long it takes

Instagram says initial review can take 3 to 4 business days. In some cases it resolves faster. If your case is flagged for further review, it can take anywhere from 1 to 8 weeks. View source ↗

What not to do

  • Don't submit five appeals. You appeal once, properly. Multiple submissions can get your case deprioritized. Source
  • Don't open a new account. Instagram can flag it as evading a ban and make recovery harder.
  • Don't pay anyone who promises to "unban" your account. Meta has taken legal action against these operations. They are scams. View source ↗

Route 2: Account Status review (restricted account)

If you can still log in but specific actions are blocked — you can't follow, like, or comment — this is a restriction, not a disable. Instagram's official Help Center attributes most restrictions to "data scraping" activity: automated access, third-party apps, or unauthorized interaction with Instagram. View source ↗

How to check your status

  • Open Instagram and go to your profile.
  • Tap the three lines menu in the top right.
  • Scroll down and tap Account Status.
  • If everything is green, you're fine. Red marks indicate a restriction — tap the arrow to see details.

How to get the restriction lifted

  • Stop the flagged activity immediately. If you were mass-following or using third-party apps, stop.
  • Remove third-party apps. Go to Settings → Apps and Websites and remove anything that automates activity. View source ↗
  • Wait 24–48 hours. Most temporary restrictions lift within this window.
  • If you see a "Request Review" button in Account Status, use it. Instagram may ask for supporting documents.

What to submit with your appeal

Instagram doesn't publish a checklist, but based on its Help Center pages and what actually works, prepare the following:

  • Access to the email or phone on the account. Instagram will send a code there.
  • A video selfie if requested. Good lighting, plain background, no hat or sunglasses. View source ↗
  • A government-issued photo ID if Instagram asks for it instead of (or in addition to) a video selfie. Accepted documents include driver's license, passport, national ID, and more. View source ↗
  • A username that matches your ID if possible. Instagram matches the document to the account, so a mismatch reads as a failed check. More on identity verification

If you're in the EU: the DSA route

If Instagram issues a final rejection with no further internal review option, and you're in the EU, you may be able to escalate to a certified DSA dispute settlement body. These bodies handle disputes involving Meta's terms or content-moderation decisions. You'll need to keep the original moderation notice and Instagram's final response. Source

This route is not a shortcut for a forgotten password or a hacked account. It's only for final moderation decisions where the internal appeal path is exhausted.

If the appeal fails

If Instagram rejects your appeal and offers no further internal review, the standard path is exhausted. Here's what you can still try:

  • Check if a new appeal option appeared. Sometimes Instagram reopens review after a period.
  • For content decisions (not account disables), the Oversight Board handles a limited number of cases. It doesn't take every submission and it moves slowly. Source
  • For EU users, the DSA dispute body route (above).
  • If the account was deleted (not disabled), Instagram says it cannot be recovered. You can create a new account with the same email, but the username may not be available. View source ↗

What doesn't work

  • Paid "recovery services." Meta has taken legal action against these operations. They are scams.
  • Repeated submissions. If your case is in the review queue, more submissions won't speed it up. They can delay it.
  • Emailing support@instagram.com. That address no longer handles account appeals.

What to do with this

If your account is disabled, use the in-app appeal button. If it's restricted, check Account Status and request a review. Submit once, accurately. Don't pay anyone. And if the appeal fails, check whether you qualify for a DSA dispute or Oversight Board escalation — but understand that those routes are slow and limited.
