How to post on Instagram: Feed, Reels, and Stories explained

12 min readUpdated September 2026

Instagram isn't one thing. It's four overlapping content channels, and each one reaches people differently. Feed posts are your permanent content. Reels get distributed to people who don't follow you. Stories reach your existing followers and disappear after 24 hours.

Here's exactly how to publish each one, with the specs you need and the rules that changed in 2026.

Feed posts: photos and carousels

Feed posts are the permanent content on your profile. They show up in hashtag and Explore results, and they're what new visitors scroll through when they land on your page.

Image sizes for Feed

Instagram's official Help Center states that the platform supports photo aspect ratios between 1.91:1 and 3:4, with a maximum upload width of 1080 pixels. If your photo falls outside that ratio, Instagram will crop it to fit. View source ↗

In practice, that means these three formats are supported:

  • Square: 1080 x 1080 pixels (1:1 aspect ratio)
  • Portrait: 1080 x 1350 pixels (4:5 aspect ratio)
  • Landscape: 1080 x 566 pixels (1.91:1 aspect ratio)

The portrait 4:5 format takes up the most vertical space in the feed, which is why many creators use it as their default. But it's a practical choice, not an official recommendation — Instagram supports the full range.

Carousels

Carousels (multiple images or videos in one post) consistently outperform single images for engagement. Every swipe is a positive signal that says "this content is worth viewing". You can include up to 20 photos or videos in a single carousel.

Reels: short-form video

Reels are the fastest-growing format on Instagram. They get distributed to people who don't follow you through the Reels tab, Explore page, and main feed — no other format gives you organic reach to cold audiences the same way.

Reels specs

Instagram's official Help Center states that Reels support aspect ratios between 1.91:1 and 9:16, with a frame rate of at least 30 FPS and a minimum resolution of 720 pixels. View source ↗

  • Aspect ratio: Between 1.91:1 and 9:16 (9:16 = 1080 x 1920 pixels, the full-screen vertical format)
  • Frame rate: At least 30 FPS
  • Minimum resolution: 720 pixels
  • Cover image: 420 x 654 pixels (1:1.55 aspect ratio). Note: the cover image can't be edited after upload.

How long should a Reel be?

Instagram allows you to record and edit Reels up to 20 minutes long. But Instagram's official Help Center states that Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. View source ↗

For maximum discovery, keep Reels under 3 minutes. Hook within the first 2 seconds. Add on-screen text because most people watch without sound.

Stories: ephemeral content

Stories reach your existing followers and disappear after 24 hours. They're for behind-the-scenes moments, polls, and casual updates that don't need to live on your profile forever.

Stories specs

  • Size: 1080 x 1920 pixels (9:16 aspect ratio)
  • Length: Up to 60 seconds per Story segment

The new hashtag rules

Instagram's official Help Center now enforces a hard limit: 5 hashtags maximum per post. If you add more, your comment fails to publish. View source ↗

This means every hashtag slot counts. The strongest strategy uses a deliberate mix:

  • 1–2 niche community hashtags — specific to your topic, with 10K–500K posts. Mid-tier hashtags consistently outperform mega-tags because your content can actually compete there.
  • 1 branded hashtag — your own community tag.
  • 1–2 topic hashtags — broader tags that describe your content category.
  • 1 format hashtag — like #reels or #carousel.

Hashtags now function as content classification signals for Instagram's algorithm, not standalone reach drivers. Choose the right five, and you're giving Instagram precise context to recommend your content to the right non-followers.

What to do with this

Use the aspect ratios Instagram actually supports: 1.91:1 to 3:4 for Feed photos, 1.91:1 to 9:16 for Reels. Keep Reels under 3 minutes — that's the official limit for non-follower recommendation. Pick 3–5 highly relevant hashtags. And remember: hashtags classify your content, they don't carry it. The content itself still does the heavy lifting.
