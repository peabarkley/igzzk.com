How the Instagram Reels algorithm actually works in 2026

12 min readUpdated October 2026

The Instagram Reels algorithm is the recommendation system that decides which Reels appear in the Reels tab, on the Explore page, and in the feeds of people who don't follow you. Unlike the Feed algorithm, which is driven mostly by who you already follow, the Instagram Reels algorithm distributes content based on how individual viewers respond to it — which is why smaller accounts can compete here in a way they can't anywhere else on the platform.

But the rules have shifted since 2025. If you're still working from older playbooks, some of what you think you know might be working against you. Here's exactly what Instagram has officially published about how the Instagram Reels algorithm works in 2026.

What Is the Instagram Reels Algorithm?

The Instagram Reels algorithm is a set of prediction models that score every Reel before showing it to a viewer. Instagram's official ranking documentation describes a pipeline where the system gathers a candidate pool, narrows it down, and then uses full prediction models to score each Reel for relevance. Those scores decide whether a Reel appears in the Reels tab, in Explore, or in someone's main feed.

The key difference from the Feed algorithm: Feed ranking is driven heavily by relationship signals — who you DM, whose Stories you watch, whose posts you comment on. Reels ranking is driven by content signals — how the video itself performs with a small test audience, regardless of whether that audience follows you. That's what makes Reels the most open surface on Instagram for reaching new people.

What the Instagram Reels Algorithm Rewards in 2026

Adam Mosseri confirmed the three most important signals for Reels distribution in January 2025: watch time, sends per reach, and likes per reach.

Watch time

How long someone actually watches your Reel. Instagram's official ranking documentation lists completion rate as one of the primary predictions for Reels distribution. A 15-second Reel watched all the way through twice outperforms a 60-second Reel that gets abandoned at 10 seconds.

Sends per reach

How often people share your Reel via direct message. For reaching people who don't follow you yet, sends carry more weight than likes. This is the signal that separates content people genuinely want to pass along from content they passively scroll past.

Likes per reach

Still one of the top three signals. For content shown to your existing followers, likes matter slightly more than sends. For discovery to non-followers, sends are more important. This distinction matters: if your goal is to reach new people, optimize for shareability. If your goal is to deepen engagement with existing followers, likes still matter.

What Changed in 2026 vs. 2025

Three shifts in the past year have changed how the Instagram Reels algorithm distributes content:

  • Recommendation Eligibility tightened. Instagram now has a formal concept called Recommendation Eligibility. If your content violates the platform's recommendation guidelines, it won't be recommended to people who don't follow you — it will only be shown to your existing followers. You can check your account's eligibility in Settings → Account Status. View source ↗
  • The definition of "unoriginal content" became explicit. Instagram's official recommendation guidelines now define unoriginal content as content that "largely reworks information from other sources, but with only minor and insignificant edits, without adding substantive value." That covers watermarked reposts, slowed-down videos of someone else's content, and screenshots of other users' posts. View source ↗
  • The 3-minute recommendation threshold was confirmed. Instagram's official Help Center now states that Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. This was previously a creator-side observation; it's now documented. View source ↗

The cold-start testing phase

When you post a Reel, Instagram doesn't immediately show it to everyone. Instagram's official documentation describes a pipeline where the system gathers potential Reels, narrows them to a candidate pool, and scores each one for relevance. A lightweight model selects approximately 100 candidates, applies integrity filters, then full prediction models score them. Reels that the system predicts will provide more value are shown sooner.

This is why smaller accounts get a real shot on Reels: the initial test pool isn't selected by follower count. It's selected by whether the content looks like something viewers will engage with. If the early test audience watches to the end and shares the Reel, distribution expands. If they scroll past in the first few seconds, distribution stops.

Why the First 3 Seconds Decide Your Reels Reach

Instagram's official ranking documentation describes a specific prediction: likelihood you'll watch less than 3 seconds of a reel — framed as a negative or "bail-out" prediction. The signals include how often viewers skip within the first few seconds of opening a Reel. Instagram explicitly models and penalizes videos with a fast bail-out rate as a first-class ranking input.

This means the opening seconds of a Reel are critical. The hook doesn't need to be flashy — it needs to make a promise the viewer wants to see fulfilled.

How Long Can Instagram Reels Be in 2026?

Instagram allows you to record and edit Reels up to 20 minutes long. But there's a catch: Instagram's official Help Center states that Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. View source ↗

Here's how the length ranges break down:

Length Recommended to non-followers? Best for
Under 15 seconds Yes Loopable content, single-idea hooks, fast retention
15–60 seconds Yes Most educational and story-driven Reels
60 seconds – 3 minutes Yes Deeper tutorials, multi-step content
3 – 20 minutes No — only shown to existing followers Long-form content for an audience that already follows you

Original audio vs. trending sounds

Instagram's official recommendation guidelines list unoriginal content as a factor that affects recommendation eligibility. The guidelines define this as content that "largely reworks information from other sources, but with only minor and insignificant edits, without adding substantive value." View source ↗

Trending audio can still work, but Reels that rely entirely on someone else's sound carry more risk than content with original audio. If you use trending audio, pair it with original content. If you use original audio, make sure the content is strong enough to stand on its own.

Originality matters more than ever

Instagram has a formal concept called Recommendation Eligibility. If your content violates the platform's recommendation guidelines, it won't be recommended to people who don't follow you — it will only be shown to your existing followers. You can check your account's eligibility in Settings → Account Status. View source ↗

In practice, that means a watermarked repost, a slowed-down video of someone else's content, or a screenshot of another user's post all fall into the category of "unoriginal content with minor edits" — and carry more risk than content you made yourself. The guidelines don't publish a complete list of every factor, but the principle is clear: adding something of substance is what makes content original.

Meme creators who add humor, social commentary, or a relatable take by incorporating unique text, creative edits, or voiceover are producing something original. The key is that you've added something that genuinely enhances it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Instagram Reels algorithm?

The Instagram Reels algorithm is a recommendation system that decides which Reels appear in the Reels tab, Explore, and other users' feeds. Instagram has confirmed that it predicts how likely you are to reshare a Reel, watch it all the way through, like it, and go to the audio page.

How long can Instagram Reels be in 2026?

Instagram allows Reels up to 20 minutes long. But Instagram's official Help Center states that Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. For discovery, keep Reels under 3 minutes.

What are the three most important signals for the Instagram Reels algorithm?

Adam Mosseri confirmed the three most important signals for Reels distribution in January 2025: watch time, sends per reach, and likes per reach. Watch time is the strongest signal for reaching non-followers.

Why am I losing followers on Reels?

Follower loss on Reels usually comes from two places: content that reaches a broad but uninterested audience, or a shift in topic that no longer matches why people followed you. The algorithm doesn't remove followers — it distributes your content to people, and those people choose whether to stay.

Why do my Reels get no views on a new account?

Instagram's ranking system tests every Reel with a small initial audience. If those viewers watch to the end, share, or rewatch, distribution expands. If they scroll past in the first few seconds, distribution stops. Low views on a new account usually means the opening seconds aren't holding attention — not that the account is shadowbanned.

What to do with this

Optimize for watch time and sends, not just likes. Keep Reels under 3 minutes — this is Instagram's confirmed ceiling for non-follower recommendation. Front-load the hook in the opening seconds. Use original audio when possible. Check your Recommendation Eligibility in Account Status. And review your Insights to see which Reels are earning sends — those are the ones the Instagram Reels algorithm is rewarding.
