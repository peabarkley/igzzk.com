Pitfalls

Account disabled? The only official appeal path

7 min readUpdated September 2026

Instagram has no general support phone number. The old support@instagram.com email no longer handles account appeals. And Meta has taken legal action against people selling fake recovery services. If your account gets disabled, you need to use the official in-app path — and you need to use it correctly the first time.

Don't do these things

  • Don't open a new account. That can be flagged as evading a ban and make recovery harder.
  • Don't submit five appeals. You appeal once, properly. Multiple submissions can get your case deprioritised.
  • Don't pay anyone who promises to "unban" your account. Meta has taken legal action against these operations. They are scams.
  • Don't share your password, OTP, or backup codes with anyone claiming they can restore your account.

The in-app appeal (preferred method)

If you see a suspension notice when you try to log in, the app itself will show an appeal button. This is the preferred method because it's tied directly to your account and goes into Instagram's internal review queue.

Here's what to do:

  • Open the Instagram app and try to log in normally.
  • When you see the notice that says "Your account has been disabled" or "We suspended your account," tap Disagree with Decision (or Appeal, depending on your app version).
  • Enter your login credentials if prompted.
  • Follow the on-screen instructions to confirm your identity. This often involves:
    • Entering the email address on your account
    • Submitting a video selfie (good lighting, face clearly visible, follow the head-turn prompts exactly)
    • Confirming a code sent to your email
  • Submit the appeal. The app will show a confirmation that your appeal is under review. Take a screenshot of this for your records.

If you don't see the appeal button

If you don't see a Disagree/Appeal button at all, your account may already be permanently disabled and past the in-app appeal window. In that case, go to the online appeal form. Many users make the mistake of skipping the in-app method and going straight to the web form. Don't. Submit the in-app appeal first, then use the web form as a follow-up if needed.

If your account was hacked

If your account was taken over rather than disabled, start at instagram.com/hacked. Follow the instructions there — this is the specific route for compromised accounts, not the general disabled-account appeal.

What to do while you wait

  • Check your email inbox and spam folder for recovery instructions from Instagram. The email will come from an official address, not a third party.
  • Avoid repeated login attempts from different devices or VPNs. This can trigger additional security flags.
  • Remove any third-party follower or automation apps linked to your account. These are a common cause of suspensions.

After you get your account back

  • Turn on two-factor authentication. This is the single most effective way to prevent future account takeovers.
  • Review your linked apps. Remove any you don't recognise or no longer use.
  • Change your password if you suspect any compromise.

What to do with this

If your account gets disabled: don't panic, don't pay anyone, and don't open a new account. Use the in-app appeal button first. Submit once, accurately. Check your email for instructions. And after recovery, turn on 2FA immediately.
