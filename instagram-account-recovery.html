Recovery

Instagram account recovery

9 min readUpdated October 2026

You can't log in. Your password no longer works, or your email was changed, or someone else has access to your account. Instagram has no support phone number, and the old support@instagram.com email no longer handles account appeals.

Here's the official recovery path, explained in plain English. Which route you take depends on one thing: what you still have access to.

The short answer

Instagram's official recovery entry point is instagram.com/hacked. From there, Instagram will guide you through password reset, login link, or identity verification depending on what you still have access to. View source ↗

If you can still access the email or phone on your account, recovery takes minutes. If you can't, it goes through identity verification — a video selfie or a government-issued photo ID — which Instagram says it reviews within 1 to 2 business days.

First, find your situation

Which route you take depends on what you still have. Find the row that matches your situation:

Your situation The route to take
You remember your password, but can't log in Password reset (below)
Your password was changed, but you still have the email Password reset link by email (below)
Your email was changed, but you still have the phone number Password reset link by SMS (below)
Someone else has access to your account Hacked account route
Email and phone are both gone Identity verification (video selfie or ID)

Route 1: Password reset (you still have email or phone)

This is the fastest route. Instagram's official Help Center walks through the exact steps: View source ↗

  • On the login screen, tap "Forgot password?"
  • Enter your username, email, or phone number
  • Tap "Send login link"
  • Complete the verification to confirm you're a real person
  • Check your email or SMS for the login link, and follow the on-screen instructions

If Instagram can't send the link to your email or phone, the Help Center directs you to use a desktop or mobile browser (not the app) to access the recovery page. View source ↗

Route 2: Your account was hacked

If someone else has access to your account — whether or not you can still log in — Instagram's official Help Center has a dedicated route: View source ↗

If you can still log in

  • Change your password (or send yourself a password reset email)
  • Turn on two-factor authentication
  • Verify the phone number and email on your account
  • Check your Accounts Center and remove any linked accounts you don't recognize
  • Revoke access for any suspicious third-party apps

If you can't log in

  • Go to instagram.com/hacked in a desktop or mobile browser
  • Select "My account was hacked"
  • Enter your username, email, or phone number to locate the account
  • Follow Instagram's on-screen instructions
  • You may be asked to submit a video selfie or a government-issued ID

Check your email first

Instagram's Help Center says to check your inbox for messages from security@mail.instagram.com. If you received a notice that your email was changed, that email contains a "secure your account" link that reverses the change. View source ↗

Route 3: Identity verification (you've lost email and phone)

If you no longer have access to the email or phone on your account, Instagram will ask you to verify your identity. There are two verification methods, and Instagram chooses which to use based on your account. View source ↗

Video selfie

Instagram will ask you to submit a short video selfie where you turn your head in different directions. This is a liveness check, not a beauty contest. Good lighting, plain background, no hat, no sunglasses. Follow the on-screen prompts at the pace asked.

Instagram says: "The video you submit will never be shown on Instagram and will be deleted within 30 days."

If Instagram can't verify you from the video, you can submit a new one and they'll review it again.

Government-issued photo ID

In some cases, Instagram may ask for a government-issued photo ID instead of (or in addition to) a video selfie. The document must show your full name and photo, or your full name with your age. View source ↗

Accepted documents include: birth certificate, driver's license, passport, marriage certificate, official name change certificate, personal or vehicle insurance card, government-issued non-driver ID, green card or immigration documents, tribal ID, voter registration, and more.

Two important warnings from Instagram

  • Don't digitally hide information on your ID. Instagram doesn't accept photos where you've obscured details through digital editing. If you want to hide non-essential information (like your ID number or Social Security number), cover it physically before taking the photo.
  • Only submit what's requested. Submitting unnecessary documents can delay the review.

After you get back in: do these four things

Recovery isn't the end. If you don't lock the account down, this can happen again.

  • Change your password. Instagram recommends at least 6 characters combining numbers, letters, and punctuation. View source ↗
  • Turn on two-factor authentication. Use an authenticator app, not SMS. Save your backup codes somewhere you'll find them. View source ↗
  • Check your login activity. In Settings, look at where your account has been accessed from, and log out any device you don't recognize.
  • Review linked apps. Go to Settings → Apps and Websites and remove anything you don't recognize. Third-party automation apps are a common cause of account compromise. View source ↗

What to do with this

If you can still access your email or phone, start with password reset — it takes minutes. If someone else has access, go to instagram.com/hacked. If you've lost both email and phone, you'll need to pass identity verification with a video selfie or a government ID. And after you're back in, turn on 2FA and save your backup codes.
