2026 Instagram update roundup: everything that changed

8 min readUpdated September 2026

Instagram changed a lot in 2026. Some updates are new features you can use. Others are rule changes that affect how your content gets distributed. Here's everything that matters, in one place.

Hashtags: 5 is now the hard limit

Instagram's official Help Center now states that you can use a maximum of 5 hashtags per post. If you add more than 5 tags to a photo or video, your comment will fail to publish. View source ↗

Hashtags now function as content classification signals for the algorithm, not standalone reach drivers. Pick 3–5 that precisely describe what your content is about. Niche community tags (10K–500K posts) outperform mega-tags because your content can actually compete there.

Original content: what Instagram's guidelines actually say

Instagram has a formal concept called Recommendation Eligibility. If your content violates the platform's recommendation guidelines, it won't be recommended to people who don't follow you. You can check your account's eligibility in Settings → Account Status. View source ↗

Instagram's official recommendation guidelines specifically list unoriginal content as a factor that affects eligibility. The guidelines define this as content that "largely reworks information from other sources, but with only minor and insignificant edits, without adding substantive value." View source ↗

The guidelines don't publish a complete list of every factor, but the principle is clear: adding something of substance is what makes content original. Meme creators who add humor, social commentary, or a relatable take by incorporating unique text, creative edits, or voiceover are producing original content. The key is that you've added something that genuinely enhances it.

First Draft: AI-powered Reels editing

Instagram launched First Draft in August 2026, first on iPhone. The feature automatically trims selected video clips, removes gaps and silent moments, and assembles the best shots into a first cut you can then refine.

The process takes under 10 seconds. It's designed for users who don't have advanced editing skills but want to produce polished Reels without leaving the app. You can access it from the Instagram camera while filming, or from the Reels gallery after selecting multiple clips.

Instagram also announced an AI-powered assistant inside the Edits app for video editing, plus a "Series" feature that lets creators link Reels together and display them in a specific order.

"Your Algorithm" topic controls

Instagram extended its "Your Algorithm" topic controls to the main Feed in June 2026, after launching them on Reels in December 2025 and Explore in April 2026. Users can now view, add, and remove the topics Instagram's AI associates with them — right in the surface where they spend the most time.

For creators, this means keyword-clear, niche-consistent Reels matter even more. Reels matched to a viewer's declared topics reach them more easily, while Reels in a topic a viewer removed get harder to surface regardless of engagement.

Keyword-rich captions outperform hashtag-heavy strategies

Instagram is shifting toward SEO and interest-based recommendations. Keyword-rich captions now outperform hashtag-heavy strategies. Write captions that clearly describe what your content is about — the algorithm uses caption text to match your content with interested viewers.

What to do with this

Use 5 hashtags maximum — that's the official limit. Make sure your content is genuinely original — "minor edits" don't count. Try First Draft if you're on iPhone to speed up Reels editing. Write keyword-rich captions instead of stuffing hashtags. And keep your niche consistent so Instagram's topic controls work in your favour.
