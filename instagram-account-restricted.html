Instagram account restricted? How to fix it

5 min readUpdated September 2026

A restricted account is different from a disabled one. When Instagram restricts you, you can still log in — but specific actions are blocked, or your content stops appearing in hashtags and search [citation:6].

The most common messages you'll see: "This action has been blocked," "Try again later," or "Action blocked" [citation:6].

What causes a restriction

Instagram's automated systems flag patterns that look like automation or spam:

  • Mass following and unfollowing. Instagram limits following to roughly 500 profiles per day, with stricter hourly limits on unfollows [citation:6].
  • Mass liking, commenting, or DMing. Rapid interactions look like bot behavior and can trigger automatic limits [citation:6][citation:20].
  • Posting too frequently with repetitive hashtags can activate anti-spam filters [citation:6].
  • Using third-party automation apps — one of the most common causes of restrictions and shadowbans [citation:6].

How to check your account status

Instagram has a built-in status checker. This is the fastest way to see if you're restricted and what's affected.

  • Open Instagram and go to your profile.
  • Tap the three lines menu in the top right.
  • Scroll down and tap Account Status.
  • If everything is green, you're fine. Red marks indicate a restriction — tap the arrow to see details [citation:20].

How to get the restriction lifted

For temporary restrictions, the fix is usually time and stopping the flagged behavior.

  • Stop the activity immediately. If you were mass-following or liking, stop. Continuing can extend the restriction [citation:20].
  • Wait 24–48 hours. Most temporary restrictions lift within this window [citation:6].
  • If you see a "Request Review" button, use it. Instagram may ask for supporting documents, and the account can be reactivated within up to two days [citation:6].
  • Remove third-party apps. Check Settings → Apps and Websites and remove anything that automates activity [citation:20].

What not to do

  • Don't keep performing the blocked action. Trying to force it can make the restriction last longer [citation:20].
  • Don't create a new account to work around it. That can be flagged as evasion.
  • Don't use the same device with automation tools after the restriction lifts — it can trigger a device ban [citation:11].

What to do with this

Check your Account Status first. If it's red, stop the flagged activity immediately and wait 24–48 hours. Remove any third-party automation apps. And don't try to force actions that are blocked — that extends the restriction.
