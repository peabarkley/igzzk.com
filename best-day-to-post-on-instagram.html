Best day to post on Instagram

8 min readUpdated October 2026

If you search for the best day to post on Instagram, you'll find dozens of articles citing different studies with different answers. Sprout Social says Tuesday and Wednesday. Buffer says Wednesday. Hootsuite says Saturday morning. Some say there is no universal answer at all.

That disagreement isn't a mistake — it's the point. Here's what Instagram officially says, what third-party data actually shows, and how to find the answer for your own account.

The short answer

Instagram doesn't publish an official best day to post. The platform's position is that the best time depends on your specific audience, and the only official tool for finding it is your own Instagram Insights. View source ↗

Third-party aggregate studies — which analyze millions of posts across thousands of accounts — generally point to Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday as the strongest days, with weekends being the weakest for most accounts. But these are averages, not rules. Your audience may behave differently.

What Instagram officially says

Instagram's Help Center does not publish a universal best day or time to post. What it does publish is a tool: Follower Activity, inside your Professional Dashboard.

Instagram's official guidance says to use your Insights to see when your followers are most active, and post during those windows. The platform's algorithm weighs early engagement heavily, so posting when your specific audience is online gives your content the best chance of gaining traction. View source ↗

That's the only official answer: your best day is whatever day your followers are most active.

What third-party data shows

The most-cited 2026 studies come from Sprout Social and Buffer. Both are third-party analyses, not official Instagram guidance. But they're useful for understanding general patterns.

Sprout Social (nearly 2 billion engagements)

Sprout Social's 2026 analysis of nearly 2 billion engagements across roughly 307,000 profiles found that Tuesdays and Wednesdays have the highest peak engagement. Weekends — especially Saturday — are the weakest for most accounts. Their data suggests the strongest windows are midweek, midday to late afternoon. View source ↗

Buffer (9.6 million posts)

Buffer's State of Social Engagement 2026 report analyzed 9.6 million Instagram posts from 200,000+ accounts. Their findings put Wednesday as the single best day, followed by Thursday and Tuesday. Evening hours (6 p.m. to 11 p.m.) consistently outperformed mornings. View source ↗

One notable exception in Buffer's data: Thursday at 9 a.m. showed surprisingly strong engagement, breaking the general evening pattern. Buffer speculates this is "end-of-week planning energy" — people checking in on creators and brands while mapping out their weekend. View source ↗

Where the studies disagree

Hootsuite's data, based on a different sample, found the best time in EST was Saturday at 11 a.m. View source ↗ Later's analysis points to early morning hours. SocialBee's analysis of 455,062 posts found Saturday had the highest overall engagement in their dataset. View source ↗

This disagreement is methodological, not random. Different studies measure different accounts, industries, and time zones. The takeaway isn't that one study is right — it's that no single day is universally best.

Why "best day" isn't a universal answer

The reason these studies disagree is the same reason Instagram doesn't publish an official answer: audiences are different.

A fitness account's audience might be most active early morning. A food account might peak at lunch. A B2B software account might see engagement during work hours, while an entertainment account peaks on weekend evenings. The "best day" for each is different.

Instagram's algorithm reinforces this: it rewards early engagement velocity. A post that gets saves, shares, and comments in its first 30 minutes will outrank one that accumulates engagement slowly. Posting when your audience is active gives you that early boost. Posting when they're not means your content dies before it has a chance.

How to find your own best day

Open Instagram and go to your Professional Dashboard → Insights → Total Followers → scroll to Most Active Times. This shows you the days and hours your followers are most active. View source ↗

Post during those windows. If your Insights shows your followers are most active on Sunday evenings, post on Sunday evenings — even if every aggregate study says weekends are weak. Your data beats their averages.

If you want a starting point before you have enough Insights data, try Tuesday through Thursday, mid-morning to early afternoon. That's where most aggregate studies converge. Then test and adjust based on what your own data shows.

What to do with this

Instagram doesn't publish a universal best day. Third-party studies disagree because audiences differ. The only reliable answer is your own Follower Activity data in Instagram Insights. Start with Tuesday–Thursday if you need a baseline, but check your Insights and trust your own numbers over any aggregate study.
