Algorithm

Instagram's 2026 algorithm, explained in plain English

8 min readUpdated September 2026

Instagram doesn't run on one algorithm. It uses several AI systems — one for Feed, one for Reels, one for Stories, and one for Explore — and each ranks content differently. Once you understand what each system actually rewards, you stop guessing and start making content that gets distributed.

The three signals Adam Mosseri confirmed

In 2025, Instagram's head Adam Mosseri publicly confirmed the three most important ranking signals on the platform:

  • Watch time — how long someone actually watches your content. This is the single most important signal across all surfaces.
  • Sends per reach — how often people DM your post to a friend. A send signals real connection, and Instagram weights it far higher than a like.
  • Likes per reach — still counted, but now the weakest of the three major engagement signals.

Likes defined Instagram's first decade. In 2026, they're the least important of the three. If your strategy still revolves around chasing likes, you're optimising for the wrong metric.

Feed vs. Reels vs. Stories: different rules for different surfaces

Feed

The Feed algorithm prioritises who you interact with most, plus how relevant and timely a post is. Relationship signals matter here — if you DM someone, comment on their posts, or watch their Stories regularly, their content appears higher in your feed. For creators, this means engagement history is your best asset.

Reels

Reels is the most open surface. The algorithm prioritises watch time and replays over follower count, which means smaller accounts can compete. The first 1–3 seconds decide reach — if you don't hold attention there, the algorithm stops testing your Reel with new audiences. DM sends are weighted 3–5x higher than likes for non-follower distribution. Keep Reels 15–45 seconds for the strongest watch-time-to-length ratio.

Stories

Stories ranks based on whose content you watch and engage with most. It's the most relationship-driven surface. If someone consistently taps through your Stories, yours will appear earlier in their tray.

Explore

Explore looks at your activity — likes, saves, comments, shares — plus popularity signals from accounts you don't follow yet. It's the discovery surface, so original content that earns saves and shares performs best here.

The shift from social graph to interest graph

Instagram used to be driven mostly by who you follow. Now it's increasingly driven by what you're interested in. The platform recommends content from accounts you don't follow, especially in Reels and Explore. Your content is no longer competing just with accounts your audience follows — it's competing on topic and relevance. Clear niches and consistent themes matter more than ever.

Original content wins

The algorithm actively down-ranks content that looks recycled from other platforms. Watermarked reposts, low-effort edits, and screenshots of other people's posts all get less distribution. Made-for-Instagram original content — photos you took, videos you filmed, memes you actually created — gets priority across every surface.

What to do with this

Stop optimising for likes. Start optimising for watch time and sends. Keep Reels short and front-load the hook. Post original content consistently. And check your Insights to see which posts are earning saves and shares — those are the ones the algorithm is rewarding.
